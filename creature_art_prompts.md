# Creature Art Prompts — *The Alchemy of Mathematical Cryptography*

Image-generation prompts for the ten creatures of the interactive grimoire textbook.
Grounded in spec.md, Q11 ("Creatures — Surfacing Community Content").

---

## Shared Style Preamble

*Prepend this (or a condensed version) to every prompt below so the ten creatures read as one set:*

> Hand-drawn creature sprite sheet in the style of an old magic textbook — a cross between illuminated-manuscript marginalia, the Voynich manuscript's otherworldly botanical-bestiary illustrations, and Deyrolle naturalist specimen plates — but rendered loosely, as if a brilliant student doodled it in colored pen in the margins during a lecture. Loose, sketchy, confident single-color ink linework on an aged parchment / cream paper background (never pure white), with light hatching and scribbled shading only. Each creature is drawn in a single accent-color ink, monochrome, with strong readable silhouettes that stay clear at very small sizes (30–40 px) and reveal charming detail at medium sizes (100–200 px). Flat, 2D, pen-on-paper — no rendering, no gradients, no digital polish.

**Sprite-sheet framing (also shared):** each prompt asks for a single sheet of ~10 small drawings of the same creature, arranged in a loose grid on one parchment page, like a naturalist's study sheet of one specimen in different poses. Consistent character across all frames; generous whitespace between frames so frames can be cut apart.

## 1. Dragon-worm — Example — Green (olive/moss)

**Primary prompt.**
A sprite sheet of a small serpentine dragon-worm, drawn entirely in olive-moss green ink on aged parchment: a long, low, horizontal wingless dragon with a scaly ribbon body, tiny stubby legs, a whiskered snout, and a curling tail — like a marginal wyrm from an illuminated manuscript crossed with a Voynich eel-creature, studied like a Deyrolle specimen plate. Loose scribbled pen linework, scales suggested by quick crescent hatching, mostly monochrome moss-green ink, no other colors. The creature is fundamentally horizontal: it coils into a compact spiral at rest and stretches out long when active. Ten small drawings of the same dragon-worm in different poses arranged on one parchment sheet, each pose reading clearly as a silhouette even when shrunk to 30–40 pixels.

**~10 frames/states.**
1. **Idle** — tightly coiled spiral, head resting on its own coils, eyes half-closed.
2. **Idle 2** — same coil, tail-tip flicked up (subtle two-frame idle loop).
3. **Alert** — head lifted from the coil, whiskers perked, one eye wide toward the viewer.
4. **Presenting 1** — body beginning to uncoil, stretching horizontally.
5. **Presenting 2** — fully stretched out long and low, like an underline beneath text, head turned back proudly toward what it reveals.
6. **Presenting 3** — stretched with a gentle S-undulation (loop frame with 5).
7. **Unreviewed (young)** — a scrappy hatchling wyrm: smaller, sparser scales, oversized eyes, energetic wiggle, a few motion scribbles.
8. **Approved (wise)** — an old wyrm: longer whiskers/beard, denser ornate scale-work, settled heavy coil, contented closed eyes.
9. **Flagged (uncertain)** — coiled loosely, head tilted, brow scrunched, a small hand-drawn question mark hovering above it in the same green ink.
10. **Settle/return** — mid-motion re-coiling, tail sweeping back into the spiral.

**Avoid:** glossy 3D render, photorealism, airbrushed digital painting, wings, fire breath, pure white background, multiple colors, thick uniform vector outlines, cute chibi style, watermark, text labels.

**Style anchors:** illuminated-manuscript marginal wyrm; Voynich manuscript creature; Deyrolle specimen plate; colored-pen margin doodle; olive ink on parchment.

## 2. Owl — Justify — Gold (ochre/amber)

**Primary prompt.**
A sprite sheet of a small round owl, drawn entirely in ochre-amber golden-brown ink on aged parchment: a compact, big-eyed, tawny-feathered owl perched on an implied branch or ledge, equal parts illuminated-manuscript margin bird, Voynich oddity, and Deyrolle ornithological study — sketched loosely in colored pen as a student's lecture doodle. Enormous round scholarly eyes are its defining feature; feathers indicated with quick scalloped hatching. Mostly monochrome amber ink, no other colors. Round silhouette at rest; leans forward intently when engaged. Ten small drawings of the same owl in different poses on one parchment sheet, every pose readable at 30–40 pixels chiefly through the round body and huge eyes.

**~10 frames/states.**
1. **Idle** — perched upright, plump and round, eyes calm and enormous.
2. **Idle 2** — slow blink, one eye half-lidded (idle loop pair).
3. **Alert** — head swiveled sharply toward the viewer, eyes at maximum width, feather tufts raised.
4. **Presenting 1** — leaning forward off its perch, neck extended, peering intently downward as if scrutinizing a claim.
5. **Presenting 2** — leaned fully forward, one wing raised like a lecturing hand, gaze piercing.
6. **Presenting 3** — slight head-tilt while leaned in (loop frame, "examining the argument").
7. **Unreviewed (young)** — a scruffy owlet: downy scribbled fluff, disproportionate eyes, hopping with eager energy.
8. **Approved (wise)** — a venerable great owl: feathery brow-tufts like spectacles or an academic's eyebrows, settled and heavy on the perch, serene knowing gaze.
9. **Flagged (uncertain)** — head cocked far to one side, eyes narrowed skeptically, a small inked question mark hovering above.
10. **Ruffle/settle** — mid-shake, feathers puffed, returning to rest.

**Avoid:** glossy 3D render, photorealism, cartoon mascot style, pure white background, multiple ink colors, blue or grey tones, smooth vector curves, watermark, text.

**Style anchors:** Deyrolle ornithological plate; illuminated-manuscript marginalia bird; amber pen scribble on parchment; naturalist field-sketch; scholarly owl doodle.

## 3. Octopus — Counterexample — Teal (deep blue-green)

**Primary prompt.**
A sprite sheet of a small alien octopus, drawn entirely in deep teal blue-green ink on aged parchment: a bulbous inky mantle with heavy-lidded strange eyes and eight sinuous tentacles, halfway between a Voynich manuscript sea-oddity and a Deyrolle cephalopod dissection plate, scribbled loosely in colored pen like a margin doodle. Its ink-ness is literal — tentacle tips trail off into loose ink scribbles and blots; suckers suggested by quick dotted lines. Mostly monochrome deep-teal ink, no other colors. Compact blob silhouette at rest; tentacles reach outward to grasp and dismantle when presenting. Ten small drawings of the same octopus in different poses on one parchment sheet, each silhouette distinct at 30–40 pixels — the round head plus radiating curling arms must never be mistaken for a bird or spider.

**~10 frames/states.**
1. **Idle** — compact blob, tentacles tucked and folded beneath, eyes half-closed, one tiny ink-drip.
2. **Idle 2** — one tentacle-tip idly curling and uncurling (idle loop pair).
3. **Alert** — eyes snapped open, mantle lifted, two tentacles raised like question-antennae.
4. **Presenting 1** — tentacles unfurling outward in all directions.
5. **Presenting 2** — full reach: eight arms extended, gripping and pulling apart imaginary pieces, dismantling an assumption; small ink splashes at the tips.
6. **Presenting 3** — arms rearranging the dismantled pieces (loop frame with 5), sly satisfied eyes.
7. **Unreviewed (young)** — a scrappy little squidlet: shorter stubby tentacles, big eager eyes, jetting motion scribbles.
8. **Approved (wise)** — an ancient kraken-scholar: wrinkled mantle drawn with dense fine hatching, barnacle-like flourishes, arms folded neatly, calm deep gaze.
9. **Flagged (uncertain)** — tentacles knotted together anxiously, eyes uneven, an inked question mark floating above the mantle.
10. **Retreat/settle** — arms folding back inward, ink cloud dissipating into a scribble.

**Avoid:** glossy 3D render, photorealism, cute kawaii octopus, bright cyan or purple, pure white background, more than one ink color, smooth gradients, horror/tentacle-monster gore, watermark, text.

**Style anchors:** Voynich sea-creature; Deyrolle cephalopod plate; teal ink blot doodle; illuminated-bestiary oddity; pen-scribble marginalia.

## 4. Firefly — Intuition — Gold (ochre/amber)

**Primary prompt.**
A sprite sheet of a tiny luminous firefly, drawn entirely in ochre-amber golden ink on aged parchment: a small elongated beetle-fly with delicate scribbled wings and an abdomen that glows — the glow rendered not as digital light but as radiating hand-drawn pen strokes, halos of short dashes and concentric scribbled rays in the same amber ink, the way a student doodles a lightbulb or a saint's halo in a margin. Part illuminated-manuscript gilded ornament, part Voynich star-diagram, part Deyrolle insect plate. It carries its own light — it IS the lantern. Mostly monochrome amber ink; the parchment left bare inside the glow so the lantern reads as brightness. Ten small drawings of the same firefly on one parchment sheet; at 30–40 pixels it must read as a tiny bright spark with wings, unmistakably smaller and lighter than the owl that shares its gold ink.

**~10 frames/states.**
1. **Idle** — at rest on an invisible surface, wings folded, abdomen giving a faint small halo of short dashes.
2. **Idle 2** — halo slightly dimmer (soft two-frame pulse).
3. **Alert** — wings flicked open, glow brightening, body lifted on tiptoe.
4. **Presenting 1** — hovering, wings a scribbled blur, glow expanding into concentric dash-rings.
5. **Presenting 2** — full radiance: large hand-drawn starburst of rays around it, the tiny body silhouetted at the center — a lantern of understanding.
6. **Presenting 3** — glow gently pulsing back and forth (loop frame with 5), a few drifting spark-dots.
7. **Unreviewed (young)** — a scrappy larva-spark: crooked wings, flickering uneven glow drawn as jittery zigzag rays, zipping motion trail.
8. **Approved (wise)** — an old steady lantern-fly: ornate lantern-like abdomen with fine hatching, calm perfectly circular halo, wings folded with dignity.
9. **Flagged (uncertain)** — glow guttering, drawn as a broken dashed halo, antennae drooping, a small question mark hovering in the dim.
10. **Ignite** — mid-flash transition frame, rays half-extended.

**Avoid:** glossy 3D render, photorealism, actual digital glow/bloom/lens-flare effects, neon yellow, pure white background, multiple ink colors, realistic insect horror, watermark, text.

**Style anchors:** gilded illuminated-manuscript ornament; Voynich star-and-ray diagram; Deyrolle insect plate; amber pen halo doodle; hand-hatched radiance.

## 5. Clockwork beetle — Applet — Green (olive/moss)

**Primary prompt.**
A sprite sheet of a small clockwork beetle, drawn entirely in olive-green ink with a verdigris-patina feel, on aged parchment: a mechanical insect whose carapace is a riveted brass-like shell (rendered only in green ink hatching), with visible gears, coiled springs for legs, a wind-up key on its back, and tiny exposed cogwork where a natural beetle would have joints. Equal parts Deyrolle coleoptera plate, illuminated-manuscript curiosity, and a da-Vinci-notebook machine sketch scribbled in a student's colored pen. Loose sketchy linework; gear teeth drawn as quick zigzags, rivets as dots. Mostly monochrome olive/verdigris ink, no other colors. Compact oval beetle silhouette at rest — clearly rounder and leggier than the dragon-worm that shares its green. Ten small drawings of the same beetle in different poses on one parchment sheet, readable at 30–40 pixels.

**~10 frames/states.**
1. **Idle** — parked, legs tucked, wind-up key still, a single idle gear visible under the shell.
2. **Idle 2** — key rotated a few degrees, one tick-mark scribble (subtle idle loop).
3. **Alert** — antennae sprung upright like unwound springs, carapace lifted slightly, gears visibly engaging.
4. **Presenting 1** — wing-cases opening like hinged panels, revealing dense little cogwork inside.
5. **Presenting 2** — fully at work: spring-legs braced, tiny tools/manipulators extended, gears mid-spin drawn with motion scribbles — tinkering, building.
6. **Presenting 3** — alternate tinkering pose, one leg adjusting a cog (loop frame with 5).
7. **Unreviewed (young)** — a scrappy prototype: mismatched loose parts, a spring popped out, exposed wiring scribbles, buzzing with eager energy.
8. **Approved (wise)** — a venerable machine: ornate engraved carapace with fine hatching, settled solidly, patina flourishes, key turning slow and steady.
9. **Flagged (uncertain)** — a gear stuck mid-turn, one antenna kinked, faint "clunk" motion mark, a small question mark hovering above the shell.
10. **Wind-down** — folding its panels shut, legs retracting to parked position.

**Avoid:** glossy 3D render, photorealism, steampunk chrome and brass rendering, metallic gradients, pure white background, colors other than olive-green ink, smooth vector art, watermark, text.

**Style anchors:** Deyrolle beetle plate; da-Vinci-notebook mechanism sketch; verdigris ink doodle; illuminated-manuscript curiosity; hand-hatched clockwork.

## 6. Court jester — Fun — Red (sealing-wax)

**Primary prompt.**
A sprite sheet of a tiny court jester, drawn entirely in sealing-wax red ink on aged parchment: a small impish human figure in a three-pointed cap with bells, a motley diamond-patterned costume (pattern suggested by quick crosshatched diamonds in the same red ink), curled slipper toes, and wildly exaggerated expressions — exactly the kind of grotesque-comic figure that capers in the margins of illuminated manuscripts, drawn loose and scribbly as a student's pen doodle, with a touch of Voynich strangeness in the proportions. Mostly monochrome sealing-wax red, no other colors. Chaotic, playful, mid-motion in almost every frame; bells drawn as little circles with motion ticks. The only humanoid in the set — its silhouette (pointed cap, splayed limbs) must read instantly at 30–40 pixels. Ten small drawings of the same jester on one parchment sheet.

**~10 frames/states.**
1. **Idle** — sitting cross-legged, chin in hands, cap-bells drooping, mischievous sidelong grin.
2. **Idle 2** — one bell twitching, one eyebrow raised (idle loop pair).
3. **Alert** — sprung to a crouch, eyes huge, cap-bells flying up, grin widening.
4. **Presenting 1** — leaping into a cartwheel, limbs splayed, bells ringing with scribbled motion arcs.
5. **Presenting 2** — full performance: mid-caper on one foot, juggling three scribbled balls (or stars), tongue out, maximum exaggeration.
6. **Presenting 3** — landed in a theatrical ta-da bow, arms flung wide (loop frame set with 4–5).
7. **Unreviewed (young)** — a scrappy apprentice fool: oversized hand-me-down cap sliding over the eyes, tripping over its own slippers, chaotic energy scribbles.
8. **Approved (wise)** — an old master fool: seated comfortably like a court sage, long cap settled, knowing half-smile, one bell polished — has earned its place.
9. **Flagged (uncertain)** — shrugging with palms up, mouth a wobbly line, bells drooping, a red question mark hovering where a juggling ball should be.
10. **Pratfall/settle** — collapsing back into the seated idle pose in a heap.

**Avoid:** glossy 3D render, photorealism, creepy horror clown, modern circus clown with makeup, pure white background, multiple ink colors, smooth vector cartoon, watermark, text.

**Style anchors:** illuminated-manuscript margin grotesque; medieval drollery figure; sealing-wax red pen doodle; motley crosshatch; capering marginalia.

## 7. Raven — Quiz — Teal (deep blue-green)

**Primary prompt.**
A sprite sheet of a small angular raven, drawn entirely in deep teal blue-green ink on aged parchment: a sharp-silhouetted corvid built from spiky, jagged pen strokes — pointed beak, ragged spiked feathers, one keen knowing eye — part illuminated-manuscript omen-bird, part Deyrolle ornithological study, with a Voynich strangeness in its stare, scribbled in a student's colored pen. Where the octopus (same teal) is all curves and the spider all thin legs, the raven is all ANGLES: wedge beak, triangular feather spikes, sharp folded wings. Mostly monochrome deep-teal ink, no other colors. It is the riddle-asker: alert, knowing, slightly theatrical. Ten small drawings of the same raven in different poses on one parchment sheet, the spiky angular silhouette unmistakable at 30–40 pixels.

**~10 frames/states.**
1. **Idle** — perched, wings folded into a sleek dart shape, one sharp eye watching sidelong.
2. **Idle 2** — feathers on the nape briefly spiking up, eye glinting (idle loop pair).
3. **Alert** — head snapped toward the viewer, beak slightly open, hackles fully spiked.
4. **Presenting 1** — hopping forward, wings half-spread in angular zigzags, beak open mid-question.
5. **Presenting 2** — full riddle pose: wings spread wide and jagged like an open book, head cocked, one claw raised as if enumerating quiz questions.
6. **Presenting 3** — leaning in close, conspiratorial, beak nearly touching the viewer (loop frame set with 4–5).
7. **Unreviewed (young)** — a scrappy fledgling: fuzzy scribbled pin-feathers sticking out at all angles, oversized beak, bouncing with impatient energy.
8. **Approved (wise)** — an ancient rook-scholar: ragged venerable feathers drawn with dense fine hatching, hunched and settled like a professor in a gown, heavy-lidded knowing eye.
9. **Flagged (uncertain)** — feathers deflated, head tilted far over, beak ajar, a teal question mark hovering above its head.
10. **Ruffle/settle** — mid-shake of the whole body, feather-spikes blurred with motion scribbles, folding back to perch.

**Avoid:** glossy 3D render, photorealism, cute round cartoon bird, soft fluffy feathers, pure white background, black ink (must be teal), multiple colors, smooth vector curves, watermark, text.

**Style anchors:** illuminated-manuscript omen bird; Deyrolle corvid plate; jagged teal pen strokes; spiky marginalia silhouette; riddling rook doodle.

## 8. Cat — Chat — Warm grey (charcoal)

Fur suggested by a few quick strokes, never fully rendered; the tail is its expressive instrument, drawn in long single confident curves. Mostly monochrome warm grey/charcoal ink, no other colors — the only grey creature in the set. It is the listener: it sits beside you, companionable and attentive rather than performing. Ten small drawings of the same cat in different poses on one parchment sheet, the eared silhouette and curving tail readable at 30–40 pixels.

## 9. Spider — Summarize — Teal (deep blue-green)

**Primary prompt.**
A sprite sheet of a small precise spider, drawn entirely in deep teal blue-green ink on aged parchment: a compact round-bodied weaver with eight thin, fine, deliberate legs and neat clustered eyes, drawn like a Deyrolle arachnid specimen plate crossed with the strange rosette diagrams of the Voynich manuscript — because its web IS a diagram: radial threads and connecting spirals drawn as fine single pen lines joining ideas together. Scribbled loose in a student's colored pen, mostly monochrome deep-teal ink, no other colors. Where the octopus (same teal) is bulbous with thick curling arms and the raven is jagged, the spider is DELICATE: a small tight body with hairline legs and hairline threads. Compact and precise. Ten small drawings of the same spider on one parchment sheet; at 30–40 pixels it must read as a neat dot with fine radiating legs, often hanging from a single drawn thread.

**~10 frames/states.**
1. **Idle** — hanging motionless from a single fine thread, legs drawn in close, compact and tidy.
2. **Idle 2** — slowly rotating on the thread, two legs delicately repositioned (idle loop pair).
3. **Alert** — legs spread wide sensing vibration, body lifted, eye-cluster gleaming toward the viewer.
4. **Presenting 1** — beginning to weave: casting the first radial threads outward in fine straight pen lines.
5. **Presenting 2** — mid-weave: at the center of a growing orb web whose radial spokes and spiral connect little nodes — a diagram of the whole section being spun together.
6. **Presenting 3** — putting in the final connecting strand, one leg extended precisely (loop frame set with 4–5), web complete behind it.
7. **Unreviewed (young)** — a scrappy spiderling: legs too long for its tiny body, dangling from a crooked thread, its practice web lopsided and scribbly.
8. **Approved (wise)** — an old master weaver: fine dense hatching on the abdomen like an ornate cushion, resting serenely at the hub of a perfect, geometric, mandala-like web.
9. **Flagged (uncertain)** — paused mid-weave before a gap in the web, two legs raised in hesitation, a teal question mark hanging in the empty space where a thread should go.
10. **Reel-in/settle** — drawing its threads back in, descending on its single line to the compact idle pose.

**Avoid:** glossy 3D render, photorealism, horror spider, hairy tarantula realism, thick heavy legs, pure white background, black ink (must be teal), multiple colors, watermark, text.

**Style anchors:** Deyrolle arachnid plate; Voynich rosette diagram web; fine teal hairline pen work; illuminated-manuscript margin weaver; diagram-spinning doodle.

## 10. Salamander — Eureka — Red (sealing-wax)

**Primary prompt.**
A sprite sheet of a small alchemical salamander, drawn entirely in sealing-wax red ink on aged parchment: a lizard-like amphibian with a curling tail, spotted back, and blunt friendly head, glowing with inner fire — the legendary fire-salamander of the alchemists as it appears in illuminated manuscripts and emblem books, studied like a Deyrolle herpetology plate, scribbled loose in a student's red pen with Voynich strangeness in its flame. The fire is drawn, not rendered: hand-inked flame-tongues, radiating dashes, and halo scribbles in the same red ink; the parchment left bare at the core of the glow so the brightness reads on paper. Mostly monochrome sealing-wax red, no other colors. Distinct from the jester (same red) by being an animal — low, four-legged, curling — never humanoid. It bursts into light when created and glows steadily once settled. Ten small drawings of the same salamander on one parchment sheet, readable at 30–40 pixels as a curled lizard wreathed in small flames.

**~10 frames/states.**
1. **Idle (settled glow)** — curled comfortably nose-to-tail, a steady small halo of short flame-dashes along its back, eyes calm.
2. **Idle 2** — the flame-dashes shifted slightly, tail-tip flame flickering (soft idle loop pair).
3. **Alert** — head raised, spots brightening, flames along the spine standing up like a crest.
4. **Presenting 1 (ignition)** — rearing up, fire swelling: flame-tongues extending outward in hand-drawn licks.
5. **Presenting 2 (eureka burst)** — full radiant burst: the salamander at the center of a large hand-inked starburst of flames and rays — the moment of discovery.
6. **Presenting 3** — flames settling from burst back toward crown-like steady fire (transition/loop frame).
7. **Unreviewed (young)** — a scrappy newt-spark: tiny, big-eyed, its flame a single sputtering candle-wisp on its tail, darting with zigzag energy lines.
8. **Approved (wise)** — an ancient fire-drake elder: heavier body with ornate spot-work in fine hatching, coiled in a dignified circle almost like an ouroboros, wearing a low steady crown of flame — it has earned its hearth.
9. **Flagged (uncertain)** — flame guttering low and drawn as broken dashes, head lowered, tail uncertain mid-curl, a red question mark hovering in the smoke-scribble above it.
10. **Settle** — folding back down into the nose-to-tail curl, fire subsiding to the steady idle halo.

**Avoid:** glossy 3D render, photorealism, realistic fire/particle effects, orange-yellow gradient flames, dragon wings, pure white background, multiple ink colors, cute cartoon axolotl, watermark, text.

**Style anchors:** alchemical emblem-book salamander; illuminated-manuscript fire-beast; Deyrolle herpetology plate; sealing-wax red pen flames; hand-hatched radiance on parchment.

## Keeping the Ten Distinct (Especially Within Shared Colors)

Because creatures share accent colors, silhouette and line-character must do the distinguishing work — the spec explicitly requires that color-sharing creatures be "unmistakable silhouettes." When iterating on generations, enforce these contrasts:

- **Teal trio — octopus / raven / spider.** Octopus = bulbous curves, thick curling arms, ink blots (soft and heavy). Raven = jagged angles, wedge beak, spiked feathers (sharp and dark). Spider = tiny compact body, hairline legs and threads (fine and delicate). Three different line weights, three different geometries; at 30 px they should be tell-apart by outline alone.
- **Green pair — dragon-worm / clockwork beetle.** Dragon-worm = long, horizontal, ribbon-like, organic scales. Beetle = compact oval, mechanical, gears and radiating spring-legs. Horizontal vs. round; organic vs. machine.
- **Gold pair — owl / firefly.** Owl = large round mass with enormous eyes, perching. Firefly = tiny spark whose drawn halo is bigger than its body, airborne. Big-and-solid vs. small-and-radiant.
- **Red pair — jester / salamander.** Jester = the set's only humanoid: pointed cap, splayed limbs, bells. Salamander = low four-legged lizard wreathed in drawn flame. Vertical figure vs. horizontal beast.
- **Cat** stands alone in warm grey — keep it the only grey creature so the Chat tool is instantly recognizable.

A practical iteration check: shrink each candidate sprite sheet so frames are ~32 px tall and confirm (a) every frame of a creature reads as that creature, and (b) no two same-color creatures could be confused. Also verify all ten sit comfortably on the same parchment tone with the same loose pen-line character, so the set feels like the work of one doodling student across one grimoire.
